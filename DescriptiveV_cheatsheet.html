Shape & Distribution

BUSINESS STATISTICS · J. ALEJANDRO GELVES
Quick Reference
01 · Position

Percentiles

Lp = 1 + k(n − 1)
Sort the data, find the location Lp, and interpolate between neighbors. k is the percentile as a decimal.
EXCEL
=PERCENTILE.INC(range, k)
02 · Any Distribution

Chebyshev's Theorem

at least 1 − 1/k²
Share of the data within k standard deviations of the mean, for any shape (k > 1).
EXAMPLE
k = 2 → 75% · k = 3 → 88.9%
03 · Bell Shaped

Empirical Rule

68% · 95% · 99.7%
Share of the data within 1, 2, and 3 standard deviations of the mean. Only for bell-shaped data.
EXAMPLE
μ = 78, σ = 6 → 95% in [66, 90]
04 · Distance

Z-Score

zᵢ = (xᵢ − x̄) / s
Distance from the mean in standard deviations. A |z| above 3 flags a suspected outlier.
EXCEL
=STANDARDIZE(x, mean, sd)
05 · Asymmetry

Skewness

Sk = n / [(n−1)(n−2)] · Σzᵢ³
Sk < 0 left-skewed, Sk = 0 symmetric, Sk > 0 right-skewed. Cubing keeps the sign of the deviations.
EXCEL
=SKEW(range)
06 · Tails

Excess Kurtosis

K = n(n+1) / [(n−1)(n−2)(n−3)] · Σzᵢ⁴
− 3(n−1)² / [(n−2)(n−3)]
K < 0 light tails (platykurtic), K = 0 like the normal (mesokurtic), K > 0 heavy tails (leptokurtic).
EXCEL
=KURT(range)
07 · Summary

Five Point Summary

Min · Q₁ · Median · Q₃ · Max
A quick picture of where the data sits and how it spreads.
EXCEL
=QUARTILE.INC(range, 0 … 4)
08 · Middle 50%

Interquartile Range

IQR = Q₃ − Q₁
The range of the middle half of the data. Extreme values do not affect it.
EXCEL
=QUARTILE.INC(range,3) − QUARTILE.INC(range,1)
09 · Outliers

IQR Method

LL = Q₁ − 1.5·IQR
UL = Q₃ + 1.5·IQR
Values below LL or above UL are outliers. This method and the z-score method can disagree.
EXAMPLE
IQ data → limits [45, 145]
